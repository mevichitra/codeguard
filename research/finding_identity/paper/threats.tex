\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\textit{Construct validity.} Our ground truth is a line diff with move detection: two findings are the same when they share a rule and the old line maps to the new one, unchanged, edited with a similarity of at least 0.6, or inside a moved block. A finding whose line is rewritten past that threshold counts as removed plus introduced. We checked 100 findings drawn in five strata from the CodeGuard replays without move detection. \ifanon One author\else The author\fi{} labelled the 60 persisting ones; an LLM rater labelled all 100 without seeing the ground truth or the human rater's labels; the human rater then reviewed the 14 findings where the LLM rater said the ground truth was wrong, and agreed on all of them. Re-running the rule showed that three of these, whole-function findings on an unchanged \texttt{def} line, were new after all. With the other 11 corrected, the line diff alone held for 59 of 60 persisting findings (98.3\%, Wilson 95\% CI 91.1--99.7) and 30 of 40 introduced ones (75.0\%, 59.8--85.8). Move detection fixes 3 of the 10 errors on introduced findings, which gives 32 of 40 (80.0\%, 65.2--89.5); the other 7 are rewrites below the threshold. It also contradicts one verdict that only the LLM rater gave, on a statement it maps as moved, and that item still needs a human ruling. Every remaining error on an introduced finding is one that some scheme matched, so hidden rates are upper bounds. In the sample the correction changes only SonarQube's rate: its 1.12\% corrects to an estimated 0.14\% (0.03--0.53), from 8 sampled findings. The 27 introduced findings the LLM rater confirmed as new had no human check, and neither Bandit's nor Semgrep's findings were sampled.

\textit{Our own tool and rater.} CodeGuard is \ifanon the authors'\else the author's\fi{} analyzer and \texttt{codeguard} is its fingerprint, so a tool whose scheme is itself measured supplies most findings, and the one human rater is also \ifanon an author\else the author\fi. Bandit's findings and Semgrep's, from 587 rules nobody in this study wrote, put the four content schemes in the same order, and on CodeGuard's own findings \texttt{codeguard} has neither the lowest false-new nor the lowest hidden rate (on Bandit's it hides the fewest). Every correction to the ground truth came from the blind LLM rater. None of this replaces a second human rater, and none has checked any item.

\textit{Internal validity.} We measure ports of each tool's identity method, not the tools, each checked against the original (Section~\ref{sec:method}). SonarQube's branch-mode move detection is modelled only for files git reports as renamed. Some CodeGuard messages contain line numbers, which penalizes methods that compare messages, so we also report Bandit's method on Bandit's own findings.

\textit{External validity.} The corpus is 35 web applications in Python and JavaScript or TypeScript. Consecutive pairs are the easy case. Our long spans chain the line diff through up to 200 commits, and the manual check covered none of them, so their hidden rates are upper bounds. A few repositories can dominate a pooled rate, so we report intervals over repositories and per-repository medians. In RQ2 each transformation is applied alone, to one finding, while real commits combine several.
